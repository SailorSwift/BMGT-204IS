# Five claims. What is wrong with each one?

NOTES: MINUTE 57. Protect this block — it is the highest-value eleven minutes of
the day. Think-pair-share for each claim: 30 seconds silent, 60 seconds in pairs,
then take answers. The answer is NOT on the slide, deliberately.

## Claim 1 of 5

> "Ice cream sales and drowning deaths rise together, so ice cream is dangerous."

NOTES: Correlation is not causation — the confounder is summer. Ask the room for
the lurking variable BEFORE you name it.

## Claim 2 of 5

> "Our new website launched in March, and Q2 sales were up 12%. So the site worked."

NOTES: Post hoc ergo propter hoc. Ask: what else happened in Q2?

## Claim 3 of 5

> "I surveyed my 40 followers and 85% prefer our brand."

NOTES: Unrepresentative sample. This plants Tuesday Sep 1 — say "hold that thought
until Tuesday."

## Claim 4 of 5

> "Sales are up 300%!"

NOTES: Small base and a missing denominator — it went from 1 unit to 4. The best
one for a business audience. Ask what the denominator was before you reveal it.

## Claim 5 of 5

> "This drug is 99% effective."

NOTES: Missing comparison — no control group. Introduces the idea of a baseline,
which is where Units 3 and 4 live.
