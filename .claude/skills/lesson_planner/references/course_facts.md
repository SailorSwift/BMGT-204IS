## The weekly rhythm — never violate it

- **Tuesday** introduces a chapter's core method: where it comes from, what it assumes, what it is for.
- **Thursday** opens with a **10-minute quiz** on the previous week, then continues the chapter and moves into Excel.
- Every deliverable is due **Tuesday at the start of class**, on Canvas.
- Exceptions with no quiz: **session 1** (Aug 27), **Exam I** (Thu Oct 8), **Exam II** (Thu Nov 12), **last day** (Thu Dec 10).

So a Thursday plan has only **65 usable minutes** after the quiz. A Tuesday plan has 75.

**The course covers a two-semester book in one semester.** The publisher's own
outline says lecture time cannot cover every topic and that students must study
on their own. A plan that tries to present a whole chapter exhaustively in 75
minutes will fail. Pick the two or three ideas that the exam and the next
chapter actually depend on, teach those properly, and point students at the
posted chapter resources for the rest — and say in the plan which topics you
are consciously leaving to reading.

## Where this schedule is tight — plan around it, don't pretend otherwise

- **Unit 5 gives one session per chapter.** Two-sample tests (Ch 10), ANOVA (Ch 11), bivariate regression (Ch 12), multiple regression (Ch 13), and chi-square (Ch 15) each get a single 75-minute meeting. The publisher outline allots two weeks apiece to several of these. Every Unit 5 plan must name the subset it teaches and what it defers to reading.
- **Ch 10 is the most compressed session in the course** — it carries two means, paired samples, and two proportions in one meeting. It is survivable only because it reuses the Ch 9 testing logic students already have. Lean on that parallel explicitly rather than re-deriving.
- **Multiple regression (Dec 3) lands five days before the final report (Dec 8).** That is deliberate but very tight. Session 27 should be built so students leave able to *run and read* a multiple regression on their own data, not merely to have seen one.
- **Exam II (Nov 12) precedes the Nov 18 "W" deadline** by six days, deliberately. Grades must be back before then.

### No datasets have been chosen yet

**The instructor has not yet decided which datasets this course will use.** The
syllabus deliberately names none, and neither should any lesson plan. When a
session needs data, state what the data must *look like* — approximate rows,
the columns and types required, what makes it teachable for that method, and
one or two candidate public sources — and leave the choice to the instructor.

The same applies to **Project 1 and Project 2**: the syllabus commits only to
their dates, points, and rubric. Their content is undecided. Do not draft
project prompts or invent project data unless explicitly asked.

anything is posted to Canvas.

## Established course conventions

Decisions already made. Later plans must not contradict them.

- `QUARTILE.INC` is the course convention, because it matches ToolPak output.
- `.S` sample variants (`VAR.S`, `STDEV.S`) are the default. The *n*−1 reason is deferred to **session 16–17**, where sampling distributions and estimation make it necessary — and that deferral is stated out loud in session 6.
- Population vs sample notation is **μ, σ, π** against **x̄, s, p**, introduced session 2 and never allowed to slip.
- Reproducibility means **live formulas, not pasted values** — worth 10 points on every project.
- **All work is individual except the Final Project.** Projects 1 and 2, quizzes, and exams are individual; the Final Project is teams of four with ±10% peer evaluation. Say which one applies whenever work is assigned.
- The semester's through-line: *what can I responsibly conclude from imperfect data?*
